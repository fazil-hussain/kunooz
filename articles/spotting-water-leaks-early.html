Spotting Water Leaks Early: Signs Every Dubai Homeowner Should Know

Hidden leaks can damage walls, floors and ceilings long before you notice a puddle. Learn the warning signs and what to do when you find one.

By Kunooz Al Asalah Team·15 September 2026·5 min read

Water leaks are one of the most common maintenance problems in apartments and villas. In multi-storey buildings a leak can also affect neighbours below. Because many pipes run inside walls and under floors, the damage often goes unnoticed for weeks. Knowing what to look for can save you a lot of money and inconvenience.

Common signs of a hidden leak

  • An unexplained rise in your water bill. Compare your recent bills. A sharp increase without a change in usage is a strong sign.
  • Damp patches or stains on walls or ceilings, often yellow or brown at the edges.
  • Bubbling or peeling paint, particularly near bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Loose or hollow-sounding tiles in bathrooms, which can mean water has got underneath.
  • A musty smell or mould in cupboards, behind furniture or around the AC.
  • The sound of running water when all taps are closed.

A quick meter test

Turn off every tap and water-using appliance in the home, then note the reading on your water meter. Wait one to two hours without using any water and check again. If the reading has moved, there is almost certainly a leak somewhere in the system.

Where leaks usually start

  1. Flexible hoses under sinks and behind toilets, which wear out over time.
  2. Water heater valves and connections.
  3. Failed grout and silicone around showers and bathtubs.
  4. AC condensate drain lines that overflow or become disconnected.
  5. Rooftop tanks and the pipes running from them.

What to do when you find one

First, shut off the water supply to the affected area. Most fixtures have an isolation valve nearby, and every home has a main stop valve. Switch off electricity to any area where water is near sockets or appliances. Then take photos for your landlord or building management and call a plumber.

Do not wait: Water that stays inside walls or under tiles can weaken plaster, damage wiring and lead to mould. A small repair today is much cheaper than replacing a bathroom floor later.
